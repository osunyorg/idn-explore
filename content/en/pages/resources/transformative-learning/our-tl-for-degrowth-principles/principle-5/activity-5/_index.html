Work towards the pluriverse

Aim: Create learning spaces that wave together diverse socio-ecological perspectives.

body: >-

Activity 5.1. - “Learning from the Pluriverse”

Level of complexity: Medium Estimated time: 3 hours
Ask participants to watch or explore one or more of the following or similar materials in advance:


Tawai: A Voice from the Forest (2017) - Indigenous philosophies and critiques of Western individualism.
Explore resources from the South Feminist Knowledge Hub and Vikalp Sangam (Alternatives Confluence)
Selected chapters: Demaria, F., Kothari, A., Salleh, A., Escobar, A., & Acosta, A. (2023). Post-development: From the Critique of Development to a Pluriverse of Alternatives. In The Barcelona School of Ecological Economics and Political Ecology: A Companion in Honour of Joan Martinez-Alier (pp. 59-69). Cham: Springer International Publishing.

Divide participants into small groups based on the resource they explored.
Ask each group to discuss:

What cultural practices, forms of knowledge, or ways of living are presented?
What values underpin these practices?
How do they challenge dominant paradigms of growth or development? 
Each group briefly presents their insights to the larger group.

Activity 5.2. - “Creative Collective Mapping: Pluriverse of Alternatives”

Level of complexity: Medium Estimated time: 2 hours
Facilitate a session where the group collaboratively creates a map (physical or digital) of diverse practices, movements, or communities around the world that embody sustainable, post-growth ways of living.
Reflect collectively: What did you learn from these diverse perspectives?
Discuss in plenary the challenges and possibilities of resisting growth dependencies through alternatives to development.
